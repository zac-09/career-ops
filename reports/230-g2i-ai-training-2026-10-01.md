## Headline caveats (read these before the score)

1. **This is not an engineering job and the report does not score it as one.** JD, verbatim: "This is not a traditional engineering role. You won't be writing production code. You'll be evaluating something harder: whether the model thinks like a great engineer." Role fit against Isaac's archetypes (Senior Backend, Staff, Platform, Tech Lead, EM, Solutions) is **2.5/5** — the role *consumes* senior engineering judgment but *produces* no shipped systems, no architecture, no team outcomes. Six months of this adds a line about AI evaluation to the CV, not a line about building. It is scored honestly as adjacent income, and the income case is made separately in §D.
2. **The $100–200/hr band is location-tiered, and Uganda is in the bottom tier.** The JD body says "$100-$200/hour depending on experience and location". G2i's syndicated postings of the *same* evaluator programme publish the tiers explicitly: **"US and Canada up to $200/hr · EU and Latam up to $150/hr · Other locations up to $100/hr"** (Built In Ottawa / Dublin / Calgary mirror of the G2i req, Aug 2026). Kampala is "other locations". Plan on **≤$100/hr**, not the headline. Prior G2i evals (#221, #222) flagged the same "based on experience and location" clause and Glassdoor's "rates appear strictly non-negotiable" signal; this req is the first where the tier is public.
3. **Spoken English on video is a hard gate, stated as such.** "Strong written and spoken English (B2 or above). You'll be writing detailed feedback and recording short video explanations, so this is a hard requirement." cv.md lists "English · Kiswahili" and an English-medium Computer Engineering degree at Makerere; four years of US/Singapore remote work corroborate the written half. The *recorded video* half has no CV evidence either way — the take-home Loom walkthrough **is** the test. Earlier syndicated versions of this req asked for "native English speaker or C2+"; this one has relaxed to B2.
4. **Hours and continuity are project-gapped.** "10-20 hours/week, flexible"; "Projects run from about two weeks to a few months each, and we offer new ones to evaluators who do well"; "Start: as soon as you clear the take-home **and a project has an open seat**." Earlier mirrors of this req (Aug 2026) say "roles are currently filled but we hire on a rolling basis". Expect idle gaps between projects and no guaranteed hours. Stability is scored on that reading.
5. **Hands-on Claude Code is Isaac's real differentiator here — but it is off-CV.** "Hands-on experience with at least one of OpenAI Codex, Claude Code or Cursor" and "Deep familiarity with modern AI-assisted dev workflows" are met in practice: this career-ops pipeline is built and operated on Claude Code daily (story-bank: "[AI-assisted engineering workflow] Career-ops agentic pipeline", "[Evidence-gated AI pipeline]"), and commit `8b6634d` (2026-09-21) is a concrete instance of reviewing and tightening an AI-assisted heuristic (`merge-tracker.mjs` role fuzzy-match). None of this is on cv.md. The tailored PDF surfaces it as a Project; cv.md itself should gain an AI-tooling line if Isaac wants to keep applying to this category.

**Take-home / Loom game plan**
- Structure every written evaluation: *verdict → the one thing that most affects the grade → evidence (quote the transcript) → what a strong engineer would have said instead → secondary issues*. Mirror the JD's own axes: makes sense / preamble useful / engineering judgment / feels right.
- Grade on the *reasoning*, not the syntax; say explicitly when an answer is "technically correct but unhelpful" — that is the phrase the JD is fishing for.
- Loom: 3–6 minutes, camera on, screen showing the transcript, talk through *why* a 2 is a 2. Record one practice run first (gap #1).

**Recommended case study:** the career-ops pipeline — specifically the evidence-gating decision and the dedup-heuristic override. It is the only artefact that shows Isaac *evaluating* agent output rather than just using it.
