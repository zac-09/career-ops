## C) Level and Strategy

**JD's level vs Isaac's natural level.** The JD's only stated bar is "5+ years of professional full-stack experience (or equivalent)." Isaac has ~6.5 years, with a team-lead stint ("Led a team of 4 developers building legal tech systems for NGOs in Uganda") and a CTO-reporting programme lead ("Led the end-to-end migration of 20+ applications … ensuring zero downtime"). He is **above** the stated bar, not at it. This is a comfortable Senior fit, and on paper he is closer to the top of the band than the bottom.

That matters commercially, because the band is $50–150/hr and is explicitly indexed on *experience* as well as location. Anchoring on experience is the only lever Isaac controls.

### Sell senior without lying

Lead with the three things the CV actually proves and the JD actually asks for:

1. **Zero-downtime delivery at scale.** *"I led the migration of 20+ production applications from Parse/MongoDB to Firebase/GCP with zero downtime, reporting to the CTO. The system stayed live the entire time — I ran two-way MongoDB↔Firestore sync over Node.js and Google Pub/Sub so both stacks served production traffic in parallel during cutover."* This is the single most senior thing on the CV and it maps to "ship performant, scalable features quickly" without needing a single word of embellishment.
2. **Commercial outcomes, not just shipped code.** *"Saved the company $5,000/month by migrating all services off AWS"* and *"Implemented Express Shipping, generating an additional $40 revenue per order."* Two hard numbers, one cost and one revenue. Most contract candidates bring neither.
3. **Typed service contracts before it was fashionable.** *"I built a USSD service talking to a Node.js backend over gRPC, and architected a Kafka/Docker/Kubernetes microservices backend."* This is the honest bridge to tRPC.

**Frame the agency model to advantage:** Isaac has been hired through a marketplace before — "Hired via Upwork to execute a complex backend migration" — and converted that contract into a permanent role at MTailor. That is *exactly* the outcome G2i sells to its clients. Say it plainly: *"I've done the contract-to-conversion path before; MTailor brought me in via Upwork for a migration and I've been there since 2022."* It de-risks him in a placement conversation better than any credential.

### If downleveled — or, here, if *rate*-leveled

There is no "downlevel" risk in the usual sense; there is a **rate-leveling** risk, which is the same problem in a different currency. See §D for the script. Structural position:

- **Accept at or above $75/hr** for a ≤6-month contract. That annualizes to ~$156K FTE-equivalent at 40h/wk and clears the $80–120K target comfortably even accounting for no benefits, no equity, and no paid time off.
- **Negotiate hard between $50 and $75/hr.** At $50/hr — the floor — a 6-month engagement is ~$52K gross with zero benefits. That clears the $60K walk-away only if the engagement runs close to full-time for the full six months, which is not guaranteed.
- **Below $50/hr: decline.** If G2i comes back below the *published floor* on location grounds, that is a signal about the relationship, not a negotiation.
- **Ask for a conversion or extension clause** rather than a title bump. The 6-month cap is the real constraint; a written "extendable / convertible on client demand" note is worth more than any label.

### Which G2i req to target — #221 or #222?

**#222 (Staff Engineer, $120–200/hr) is the better target. Apply there first.**

Even a stretch application at Staff, discounted to the bottom of its band, pays more than a comfortable application at Senior paid at the top of its. And the geographic-discount risk — the thing that most damages #221 — hurts far less when the floor is $120/hr.

Mention #221 in conversation as an acceptable alternative if they level him down — that way the fallback costs nothing and the anchor stays high. Do **not** submit two separate cold applications on the same day; it reads as spray, and with an agency the recruiter sees both.

### Reading the number honestly

The $50–150/hr band is real but it is a *marketplace* band, and the external evidence is unambiguous that G2i prices by geography. The Tier 3 data point — **$50/hr US vs $35/hr "in most other countries"**, a 30% haircut — is the clearest possible statement of company policy. Applied to this req, the honest expectation for a Kampala-based candidate is the **bottom third of the band**, i.e. $50–70/hr, with real risk of an offer *at* or *below* the published floor.

**What that means in money:**

| Scenario | Rate | 6-month gross (40h/wk, 26wk) | Annualized FTE-equivalent | vs targets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Floor, location-discounted | $50/hr | ~$52,000 | ~$104,000 | Below $60K minimum **for the engagement**; annualized rate clears target |
| Realistic mid | $70/hr | ~$72,800 | ~$145,600 | Clears $60K floor; clears $80–120K target |
| Experience-anchored | $100/hr | ~$104,000 | ~$208,000 | Well clear |
| Discounted below floor | $35/hr | ~$36,400 | ~$72,800 | **Decline** |

The critical distinction: **the hourly rate looks excellent against Isaac's targets; the engagement total does not necessarily, because it stops at six months.** `config/profile.yml` sets a target of `$80K-120K` and a minimum of `$60K` — those are annual figures. A 6-month contract at $50/hr does not reach the floor as a *year's income* unless it converts, extends, or is immediately followed by other work. Comp scored on that basis.

### ⚠️ Caveat 1 — Location-indexed pay (the headline risk)

The JD says the rate is set "based on experience **and location**." G2i has published tiers that price non-US developers ~30% below US developers for identical work. This is not speculation about a policy; it is the policy.

**Script — geographic-discount pushback (from `modes/_shared.md`):**

> **"The roles I'm competitive for are output-based, not location-based. My track record doesn't change based on postal code."**

Deploy it **after** they name a number, never before. Supporting frame, in Isaac's own facts:

> *"I've been delivering for US companies from Kampala since 2020 — MTailor, Mind2matter — and for a Singapore company before that. I migrated 20+ production apps with zero downtime and took $5,000/month off the infrastructure bill. That work was priced on what it was worth, not on where I sat."*

And the direct ask, using the band's own words against it:

> *"The posting says the rate is based on experience and location. I'd like to talk about the experience half. What does the $150 end of the band look like, and what would I need to show to sit in the upper half of it?"*

This reframes the conversation onto the axis Isaac can win on, without accusing anyone of anything. If they will not move off a location band, the fallback is **structure rather than rate**: fewer guaranteed hours at a higher rate, or an extension/conversion clause, both of which cost the client less than a headline rate increase.

### ⚠️ Caveat 2 — Contract vs full-time mismatch (unresolved)

The Ashby chip says `Employment Type: Full time`. The JD body says "**Contract** roles available, up to 6 months." These cannot both be the whole truth, and the difference is worth tens of thousands of dollars and all of the stability.

Scored on the body — ≤6-month contract, no benefits, no equity, no notice protection. But **this must be the first question at screening**, before comp:

> *"The posting lists this as full-time on the board but the description says contract up to six months. Which is it for this req — and if it's contract, is there an extension or conversion path when the engagement ends?"*

Ask it neutrally and early. The answer changes the whole valuation: a full-time placement at even $50/hr-equivalent is a ~$104K/yr role with continuity, which would move Comp from 2.8 to roughly 4.0 and lift the overall score by ~0.25.

### Demand

React + Node.js full-stack contracting remains liquid, and the AI-tooling niche G2i is staffing ("data tools, operational dashboards … power the AI frontier") is where the current contract budget actually sits. G2i itself has been running $200/hr AI-code-evaluation campaigns. Demand is not the constraint here; pricing is.

**The Parse → Firebase migration, told as a delivery story rather than an infrastructure story.** Structure it as: the constraint (business must stay live), the mechanism (Pub/Sub two-way sync as a bridge), the sequencing (20+ reversible cutovers instead of one), the numbers (zero downtime, $5,000/month saved).
