## C) Level and Strategy

**JD level vs natural level.** The JD is unbanded. Isaac's natural level for a Node.js backend seat is solid senior. For *this* seat — a protocol-and-algorithms pod — his effective level on the team's own axis is entry-to-mid, because the domain knowledge is not there yet. That mismatch is the whole story of this evaluation.

**Sell senior without lying.** The honest senior signal here is not "I know P2P" — it is *"I have built the hard parts of distributed data movement in production, and I am converting that into P2P specialism deliberately."* Concretely:
- Lead with the **two-way MongoDB↔Firestore sync** (cv.md L16). Frame it in the JD's own vocabulary: two authoritative replicas accepting concurrent writes, convergence under partition, conflict handling, cutover with no downtime. Then say the honest thing — "I solved that with a parallel write path and Pub/Sub ordering; CRDTs are the principled version of the same problem and that's what I want to go deep on."
- Second, the **Kafka + NATS Streaming + gRPC microservices** at CodeBits (cv.md L77) — event-driven distributed design, four NGO tenants, independent deploys.
- Third, **shipping bias** — the JD explicitly asks for "a bias for building" over research purity, and Isaac's record is unambiguously a shipper's ($40/order Express Shipping, $5K/month infra savings, cv.md L19–L20). This is a real edge against a pure-researcher applicant pool, and it is the one dimension where he may beat the modal candidate.

**The application strategy that actually matters.** For this req specifically, the CV is not the lever — the artifact is. Recommended sequence: (1) build the small hyperswarm/hyperbee demo, (2) build the BM25 inverted index toy, (3) write ~600 honest words on what surprised you about DHT lookup behaviour, (4) *then* apply with links in the first paragraph. Applying without these is a low-cost lottery ticket; applying with them is a real candidacy. If Isaac will not do (1)–(3), the better use of the same hour is the Tether Wallets req (#217) or another Bitfinex-shaped backend seat.

**If downleveled.** Unlikely to be offered a downlevel here — teams like this hire in-domain or not at all. If a more generalist Node.js seat elsewhere at Tether/Holepunch is offered instead, take it: it is the legitimate on-ramp into exactly this codebase, and #082 showed that door scores highest of the three.
