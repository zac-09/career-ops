**Gaps and mitigation:**

1. **Apache Airflow — hard-ish gap, but the cheapest to close.** The JD names it flatly under "What you'll bring". Mitigation: this is a genuine weekend-to-two-weeks ramp — stand up a local Airflow, port the Dr Wealth "ad-hoc jobs" pattern into a scheduled DAG with retries/backfill, and reference it honestly as "I orchestrate these by hand today; here is the same pipeline as an Airflow DAG." Do that *before* the take-home, since a data-engineering take-home will very likely be pipeline-shaped. **Do not list Airflow on the CV.**
2. **Apache Spark — the real blocker.** Distributed compute is not adjacent to anything on the CV; Pub/Sub message-at-a-time processing is a different mental model from partitioned batch compute. Not fakeable in two weeks to a level that survives a 90-minute technical interview. Mitigation: concede it explicitly in the cover letter and trade it against the near-real-time/event-driven strength, which the JD also asks for and which most Spark-first candidates lack. Expect it to cost him against a career data engineer.
3. **Warehouse / dimensional modelling + Redshift/Athena/EMR.** "Data warehouses, operational data stores, data integration projects" — he has the third of those three, convincingly, and neither of the first two. Mitigation: lead with "data integration projects" wording, which is exactly what a 20+ app cross-datastore migration is, and do not overclaim the warehouse half.
4. **Relational at production scale.** SQL is a Proficient skill with no RDBMS named in any role. Mitigation: be honest in the screen — deep NoSQL at multi-million-record scale, competent SQL, ramping on analytic SQL. A SQL screen is plausible at the take-home stage; this is worth an hour of window-function/CTE practice regardless of this application.
5. **AWS-vs-GCP inversion.** His strongest cloud story is leaving AWS for GCP. Mitigation: reframe as cloud-agnostic data plumbing — S3↔GCS, Pub/Sub↔SNS/SQS/Kinesis, Firestore↔DynamoDB are the same primitives. ## The B2B / B2C question — read

The form's required "Are you happy working with Sporty under a B2B or B2C arrangement?" is not a trick and is not a downgrade signal. It is the standard mechanism a globally-distributed company uses to hire in countries where it has no legal entity — and it is precisely **why Uganda is selectable in the location dropdown at all.** Sporty has no Ugandan entity; B2B is how a Kampala-based hire actually gets papered. Answering "yes" is what keeps the application viable.

**What it costs Isaac:** no employer-side benefits, no severance, self-managed Ugandan tax and social contributions, equipment and downtime on his own account, and no unemployment backstop. The "28 days paid annual leave" line in the JD is almost certainly an employee benefit that a B2B contract will not carry — worth confirming explicitly in the TA screen, along with whether the quarterly performance bonus applies to contractors. **What it buys him:** currency-flexible invoicing, cleaner geographic-arbitrage economics, and legitimate access to a role that otherwise would not exist for someone outside Sporty's entity footprint.

**Answer "Yes — happy with either; B2B is straightforward from Uganda", then price it accordingly.** The mistake to avoid is answering "yes" to B2B while quoting an *employee* salary number in the salary-expectations field two questions earlier — that silently accepts a 30–50% real-terms cut. Quote both numbers, in one sentence, as drafted in section D. Also ask in the screen: invoicing currency, payment cadence and FX handling, notice period on both sides, and equipment provision (the JD promises "top of the line equipment" — confirm that survives a B2B arrangement).
