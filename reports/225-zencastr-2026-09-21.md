**Gaps — honest triage:**

1. **"5+ years specifically in data engineering" — HARD BLOCKER.** Not mitigable by cover letter. The word "specifically" is there to filter out exactly this profile: a strong backend engineer who has touched data. A recruiter screening on this line rejects at the first pass.
2. **"Built and owned a dimensional model in production" — HARD BLOCKER.** This is the single thing the role exists to do, and there is no version of Isaac's history that supplies it. A side project would not substitute for "in production".
3. **dbt + orchestrator + cloud warehouse — three-way core gap.** Any one alone would be a learnable ramp. All three missing at once removes the entire day-to-day toolchain. Mitigation only exists on a multi-month timeline (build a dbt + BigQuery + Dagster project on real data), not for this application.
4. **SQL depth.** Listed as Proficient but with zero analytical-SQL evidence on the CV; in a data-engineering loop this is tested directly and hard. Would likely be screened out on a SQL exercise.
5. **Geo unstated.** Not a skills gap but a process gap — see the recommended action.

**What is genuinely strong and should not be undersold:** ingestion and event streaming (Pub/Sub named in the JD and on the CV), CDC-in-substance via live two-way sync, inheriting and operating other people's systems, cost/performance optimisation, and stakeholder translation. That is roughly four of the thirteen "good fit if" bullets — real, but it is the supporting cast, not the lead.
