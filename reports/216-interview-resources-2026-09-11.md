2. **Deep SQL / Postgres.** "Indexes, transactions, and what happens under concurrent writes" is a specific, testable bar, and Isaac's depth is Firestore and MongoDB. Note the partial credit he genuinely has: concurrency, ordering and redelivery under live traffic is exactly what the Pub/Sub sync and the Kafka services were about — he has the *reasoning*, in the wrong engine. Mitigation: say so plainly rather than bluffing, and spend 1–2 weeks before the take-home on Postgres specifics (EXPLAIN ANALYZE, B-tree vs partial vs composite indexes, MVCC, isolation levels and what actually happens on concurrent UPDATE, `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, advisory locks, and safe online migrations — expand/contract, `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY`, `NOT VALID` constraints). Drizzle on top of that is a weekend.
3. **"…and you can explain how you measured it."** The WhatsApp OCR chatbot clears "built something on an LLM API that had to be correct" — prescription OCR is high-stakes and it shipped. It does **not** clear the measurement half: there is no recorded ground-truth set, accuracy figure, or regression suite. Treat this as half a pass. Mitigation, in order: (a) Isaac reconstructs honestly how OCR output *was* validated in production — even "a human pharmacist confirmed every extraction before it was used" is a real correctness mechanism and a good answer; (b) before the systems call, build a small eval harness over career-ops (a labelled set of ~30 postings, measure geo-label precision against the Playwright-verified truth) — that converts the biggest gap into the freshest story and is a few hours of work; (c) never claim a number that does not exist.

That is not a small set, and it is why this scores as a genuine fit on the work despite four ❌s.
