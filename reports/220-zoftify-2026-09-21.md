## C) Level and Strategy

**Detected level in JD:** Mid-level, explicitly, twice (`Minimum Experience: Mid-level`; "3+ years of Node.js").
**Isaac's natural level for this archetype:** Senior. `profile.yml` sets `target_roles.primary: Senior Backend Engineer / Senior Software Engineer` and `archetypes[0].level: Senior/Staff`. `cv.md` supports it: ~6.5 years continuous Node.js, "Led a team of 4 developers" at CodeBits, "Led the end-to-end migration of 20+ applications… reporting directly to the CTO" at MTailor, and architecture ownership ("Architected a microservices backend… using Apache Kafka, Docker, and Kubernetes").

**This is the central tension of the application: the req is one band below the candidate.**

### The Backend Team Lead req on the same board

The scanner's `title_filter.positive` missed a **Backend Team Lead** opening on this same BambooHR board. That is almost certainly the better-targeted req for Isaac, and it matters more than anything else in this evaluation:

- It matches `_shared.md` archetype **"Backend Tech Lead"** — "Team leadership, code review, architecture decisions… Someone who leads a backend team technically."
- Isaac has the direct evidence a mid-level req cannot use: **"Led a team of 4 developers building legal tech systems for NGOs in Uganda"** (CodeBits, 18 months) and **"Architected a microservices backend"** — plus "Wrote documentation for fellow engineers on working with the new Firebase SDKs" and "Trained the Ops team on the new Firebase Dashboard and backup procedures", which is mentorship and enablement, not just delivery.
- A Team Lead band at a 30-person agency plausibly clears $60–80K where the mid-level band plausibly does not. **The comp problem below is substantially a level problem, and the lead req is the direct fix.**

**Action: pull the Backend Team Lead req into the pipeline as its own evaluation before sending anything to req 30.** Also fix the scanner — `portals.yml` `title_filter.positive` should include `team lead`, `tech lead`, `lead engineer`, `engineering lead`. This is the second time in the pipeline that a lead-titled req has been filtered out.

### "Sell senior without lying"

The honest framing is **not** "I'm too senior for this" — it is *"I clear your bar on day one on the stack, and I bring the things in your advantage column already done."* Concrete phrases, each tied to a real CV line:

1. **Open on the scale differential, not on years.** "You're asking for 3+ years of Node. I've shipped Node in production continuously since January 2020 across five companies on three continents." (Every Stack line on `cv.md`.)
2. **Lead with the multi-cloud line, because it covers three of their five advantage bullets at once.** "Your advantage list names Docker/Kubernetes, AWS/Azure/GCP including Firebase, and NoSQL. I led a zero-downtime migration of 20+ production applications from Parse/MongoDB to Firebase/GCP, built the MongoDB↔Firestore two-way sync on Google Pub/Sub that kept both systems live during cutover, moved file storage from S3 to GCS, and took $5,000/month off the infrastructure bill." One paragraph, four advantage bullets, one hero metric.
3. **Match their stated identity.** They say "Unlike outsourcing firms, we focus on delivering business value… and take responsibility for the solutions we build." Answer in their own currency: "$40 additional revenue per order from Express Shipping. $5,000/month saved. Increased buyer conversion from the 3D visualisation feature." This is the single highest-leverage move in the application — it is precisely `_shared.md`'s "entrepreneurial / ownership / builder / end-to-end → #1 differentiator, increase match weight".
4. **Architecture, stated plainly once.** "I've architected a microservices backend on Kafka, Docker and Kubernetes, and led a team of four building it." Say it once, without pressing — at a mid-level req, pressing reads as a flight risk.
5. **Timezone as an asset.** "I'm at UTC+3 — the Tallinn clock. Full overlap with the team, and I've covered US hours from Kampala for four years at MTailor."

### "If I get downleveled" plan

Assume the mid-level band *is* the offer. Then:

- **Accept only if the number clears `$60K` (`profile.yml` minimum).** Below that, this is a net-negative move against a current US-remote role, regardless of stack fit.
- **Negotiate title, not just money.** "Senior Backend Developer" at no extra cost to them is worth more to Isaac's next application than $3K. Agencies of 30 people are usually flexible on titles because titles are client-facing.
- **Bank the built-in review cadence.** The JD offers "Performance reviews" and "Bonuses" as standing benefits — ask for the first review at **6 months, with written criteria**, and name the criteria yourself: owning a client project end-to-end, and shipping the first NestJS/Postgres service.
- **Take the training benefit in writing.** "Skill development, including paid training and courses" is a real asset here: it is the funded path to formal TypeScript/NestJS/Postgres credentials, which converts this role's two gaps into two strengths within a year.
- **Price the contractor structure in.** If the Estonian entity engages Isaac B2B rather than on payroll, `_shared.md` applies: "Contractor rates are typically 30-50% higher than employee base to account for benefits." Uganda-side tax and no Estonian benefits both land on him — quote accordingly, and note that the JD's benefit list (paid vacation, health days, birthday off) may or may not survive a contractor conversion. **Ask which structure is on offer before quoting a number.**

## Full-Stack (#219) vs Backend (#220) — Verdict

Both reqs sit on the same BambooHR board and Isaac should send **one** application, not two. Sending both signals indifference to fit, which is exactly what `CLAUDE.md`'s ethical-use section warns against.

**#220, this req, is the better fit.** The reasoning:

1. **Archetype.** `profile.yml` ranks `Backend Engineer` as `fit: primary` and `Full-Stack Engineer` as `fit: secondary`. The primary archetype should win when both are available on the same board on the same day.
2. **Strength concentration.** Isaac's hero material is *entirely* backend: the 20+ app zero-downtime migration, the Pub/Sub two-way sync, 2M+ Firestore records, Kafka/Docker/Kubernetes microservices, gRPC, $5,000/month saved. His React lines are real but thin by comparison — "Added responsive UI pages… using HTML, Tailwind, and React" and "Delivered React UIs from Figma designs" are execution bullets, not ownership bullets. A full-stack req invites evaluation on the weaker half.
3. **Advantage-column sweep.** The backend req's advantage list (Docker/K8s, AWS/Azure/GCP/Firebase, NoSQL) reads like an index of `cv.md`. A React-inclusive req dilutes that surface with frontend requirements where Isaac has no differentiating evidence.
4. **Trajectory.** Backend-only is the move that compounds toward `Senior Backend Engineer` / `Backend Tech Lead`. Full-stack at an agency broadens the résumé in the direction Isaac is deliberately narrowing.

**The one genuine argument for #219** is that full-stack reqs at small agencies are often banded and paid higher than pure-backend mid-level seats, because the seat covers more ground — and comp is this application's binding problem. That argument does not survive contact with the better option: **the Backend Team Lead req solves the comp problem without costing archetype alignment.**

**Final ordering: Backend Team Lead (unevaluated, pull it in) > #220 Backend Developer (Node) > #219 Full Stack.**
